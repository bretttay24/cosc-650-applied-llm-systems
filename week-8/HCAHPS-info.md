# HCAHPS Background and Topic Definitions

## What HCAHPS Is

The Hospital Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems
(HCAHPS, pronounced "H-caps") Survey is the national, standardized, publicly
reported survey of patients' perspectives of hospital care. The Centers for
Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) uses a standardized survey instrument and
administration methodology so that results can be compared across hospitals.

Official HCAHPS results come from structured survey responses. CMS aggregates
and adjusts those responses before publishing hospital results. Hospitals may
ask supplemental questions after the required HCAHPS items, and healthcare
organizations may also collect narrative comments through separate
patient-experience programs.

This reference defines eight HCAHPS-aligned themes concerning specific parts
of the hospital experience. It does not define themes for the two global
HCAHPS measures:

- Overall hospital rating
- Willingness to recommend the hospital

Those global measures ask patients for structured assessments of the hospital
as a whole rather than about a specific care-experience topic.

## Topic Labels

| Label | Primary focus |
| --- | --- |
| `communication_nurses` | How nurses treated, listened to, and explained information to the patient |
| `communication_doctors` | How doctors treated, listened to, and explained information to the patient |
| `staff_responsiveness` | How promptly staff provided requested help |
| `communication_medicines` | Explanations about a new medicine before or while it was given during the stay |
| `discharge_information` | Information about help needed and warning signs after discharge |
| `care_transition` | Whether the patient was prepared to manage care after leaving |
| `cleanliness` | Cleanliness of the patient room and bathroom |
| `quietness` | Noise around the patient room, especially at night |

## `communication_nurses`

### Meaning

Comments about how nurses communicated and interacted with the patient.

### Includes

- Courtesy and respect shown by nurses
- Whether nurses listened carefully
- Whether nurses explained care in an understandable way
- Nurses dismissing questions, interrupting, or speaking disrespectfully

### Does not include

- Waiting a long time for a nurse or requested assistance
- A nurse failing to explain a newly administered medicine
- General praise that does not identify a nursing interaction

### Example

> The nurse slowed down, listened to my concerns, and explained the procedure
> in words I could understand.

## `communication_doctors`

### Meaning

Comments about how physicians communicated and interacted with the patient.

### Includes

- Courtesy and respect shown by doctors
- Whether doctors listened carefully
- Whether doctors explained diagnoses, tests, or treatment clearly
- Confusing, contradictory, rushed, or dismissive physician communication

### Does not include

- Communication by nurses or unspecified hospital staff
- Explanations specifically about a new medicine given during the stay
- Whether staff responded promptly to a request

### Example

> The physician answered every question but used so much medical terminology
> that I still did not understand the diagnosis.

## `staff_responsiveness`

### Meaning

Comments about how quickly hospital staff provided requested help.

### Includes

- Delays after pressing a call button
- Waiting for help getting to the bathroom or using a bedpan
- Waiting for requested assistance or pain medicine
- Staff promising to return with help but taking an unreasonable amount of
  time

### Does not include

- Rude communication when help was otherwise provided promptly
- Whether nurses or doctors explained care clearly
- General delays that do not involve a request for help from hospital staff

### Example

> I pressed the call button twice and waited almost an hour for help getting
> to the bathroom.

## `communication_medicines`

### Meaning

Comments about whether hospital staff explained a new medicine before or
while giving it during the hospital stay.

### Includes

- What a new medicine was for
- Possible side effects of a new medicine
- Explanations that were missing, unclear, or difficult to understand
- Medicine explanations by a nurse, doctor, pharmacist, or other hospital
  staff member during the stay

### Does not include

- Understanding the purpose of medicines after leaving the hospital
- Delays bringing requested medicine
- General physician or nurse communication unrelated to a new medicine

### Example

> They added a new heart pill during my stay but never explained why I was
> taking it or what side effects to report.

## `discharge_information`

### Meaning

Comments about concrete information the patient received regarding help and
warning signs after leaving the hospital.

### Includes

- Whether staff discussed the help the patient would need at home
- Whether family members or caregivers received relevant instructions
- Information about symptoms or health problems to watch for
- Instructions about when to call a clinician or return for care

### Does not include

- Whether the discharge plan accounted for the patient's preferences or
  living situation
- Whether the patient felt capable of managing care after leaving
- Understanding the purpose of take-home medicines

### Example

> Before discharge, no one told us which symptoms meant I should call the
> doctor or return to the hospital.

## `care_transition`

### Meaning

Comments about whether the patient was prepared to manage health and care
after leaving the hospital.

### Includes

- Whether the discharge plan considered the patient's needs and preferences
- Whether family or caregiver needs were considered
- Whether the patient understood personal care responsibilities
- Whether the patient understood the purpose of medicines taken after
  discharge
- Whether the plan was realistic for the patient's home situation or
  abilities

### Does not include

- A missing explanation about a new medicine given during the stay
- Written warning signs or instructions about when to seek help
- General dissatisfaction with the discharge process

### Example

> The plan assumed I could manage the wound by myself even though I told them
> I have limited use of my hands and live alone.

## `cleanliness`

### Meaning

Comments about the cleanliness of the patient's room and bathroom.

### Includes

- Dirty floors, surfaces, bedding, or bathrooms
- Trash, spills, odors, or visible contamination related to cleanliness
- Whether the room and bathroom were cleaned adequately
- Positive comments specifically describing a clean room or bathroom

### Does not include

- Noise around the room
- Room temperature, bed comfort, food, or other amenities
- Clinical infection concerns without a comment about environmental
  cleanliness

### Example

> The bathroom floor stayed sticky for two days even after I asked for it to
> be cleaned.

## `quietness`

### Meaning

Comments about noise around the patient's room, especially noise that
affected rest at night.

### Includes

- Loud staff conversations
- Hallway noise, alarms, carts, televisions, or nearby rooms
- Repeated nighttime disruptions caused by environmental noise
- Positive comments specifically describing a quiet environment

### Does not include

- Necessary clinical care described without a noise concern
- Room cleanliness, temperature, lighting, or bed comfort
- A noisy location outside the hospital stay

### Example

> Staff conversations and carts in the hallway kept waking me throughout the
> night.
